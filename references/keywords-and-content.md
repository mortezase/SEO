# Keywords and content

## Demand and intent

Use real query data when available. Group queries by shared intent and required page outcome, not word similarity alone. Identify informational, commercial, transactional, navigational, local, and mixed intent, while noting SERP features and ambiguity.

Map one primary intent cluster to the page best able to satisfy it. Flag cannibalization only when multiple URLs compete for substantially the same task and evidence shows confusion or dilution.

## Content planning

A useful brief includes audience, search task, business goal, primary and supporting questions, entity coverage, evidence requirements, differentiated angle, outline, internal-link opportunities, conversion path, and success measure.

Do not produce content merely to cover keywords. Require original value such as first-hand experience, data, tools, examples, comparisons, expert review, or a clearer solution.

## On-page optimization

Write accurate titles and descriptions that reflect the page and invite the right click. Use one clear main heading and a logical heading structure. Keep the primary answer discoverable, support it with depth appropriate to the task, and use descriptive links and image alternatives.

Avoid keyword stuffing, templated filler, unsupported claims, fake expertise, invented citations, and mechanically adding FAQs. Update or consolidate weak pages when that better serves users than publishing another URL.

## Content review

Evaluate intent satisfaction, factual support, information gain, authorship and accountability, freshness where relevant, clarity, accessibility, internal links, conversion alignment, and duplication. Treat E-E-A-T as a quality and trust framework, not a field that can be filled with boilerplate.
